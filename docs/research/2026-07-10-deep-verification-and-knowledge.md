## Part II — Knowledge Briefings

# CLV and Devigging — Deep Briefing

## 1. Why the sharp closing line is the efficiency benchmark

**The established case.** The closing line aggregates the maximum information: every model, every sharp order, every injury/lineup leak has had a chance to move the price by kickoff. Pinnacle's own resources frame beating the close as the primary skill test — positive CLV is "one of the strongest indicators that your betting strategy is sharper than the general public's," and consistently achieving it is what gets accounts limited at recreational books (https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/educational/what-is-closing-line-value-clv-in-sports-betting). Buchdahl's football-data work operationalizes the same claim: Pinnacle's margin-stripped odds "do a phenomenal job of replicating the 'true' probabilities of outcomes," which is precisely why the ratio of your taken price to the devigged close is a usable proxy for expected profit (https://www.football-data.co.uk/The_Wisdom_of_the_Crowd_updated.pdf; https://www.football-data.co.uk/blog/wisdom_of_crowd_betting_system_closing_odds.php). Štrumbelj (2014, *Int. J. Forecasting*) adds the academic anchor: betting odds are "the most accurate publicly-available source of probability forecasts for sports" — with the important caveat that **bookmakers differ significantly as probability sources, and exchange odds are not always best, especially in small markets** (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207014000533). The statistical appeal is speed: CLV separates skill from noise in ~50–100 bets where ROI needs thousands (Pinnacle, above; also Walsh & Joshi 2024 on calibration→profit, https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021).

**The evidence against perfect closing efficiency (2017–2026).** This is where folklore ("the close is efficient, full stop") diverges from the record:

- **Simon (2024), *Management Science* 70(12):8583–8611** — "Inefficient Forecasts at the Sportsbook" — tested open-to-close line movement at four sportsbooks across 3,681 MLB games. Forecasts are "mostly reliable, but there are simple betting strategies that would have yielded significant profit"; forecasts **do not improve monotonically as the game approaches**, and forecasts at weekend day-game start times are *significantly worse than 90 minutes earlier*. Sequences of within-game line moves show these markets "do not incorporate information optimally" (https://aura.american.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Inefficient_Forecasts_at_the_Sportsbook_An_Analysis_of_Real-Time_Betting_Line_Movement/30546293; https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v70y2024i12p8583-8611.html). Operational implication: "the close" is not even the pointwise-best forecast at some books/times — a *timestamped sharp* close is a choice, not a given.
- **Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner (2017)** beat football books using only consensus **closing** odds as the truth proxy and betting outlier prices — profitable in a 10-year closing-odds simulation, a minute-level simulation, and 5 months of real money, terminated by account limiting, not by alpha decay (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02824). This simultaneously *supports* the close-as-benchmark (the consensus close identifies mispriced outliers) and *refutes* strong-form efficiency of soft books' closes.
- **Angelini & De Angelis (2019), *Int. J. Forecasting* 35(2):712–721** find heterogeneous efficiency across 41 bookmakers and 11 European leagues — some bookmakers' closing prices show systematic mispricing exploitable in-sample (https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3070329; https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p712-721.html).

**Synthesis (established):** the *sharp* close (Pinnacle/Betfair-class) is the best cheap benchmark and the fastest skill signal; it is **not** an oracle. Efficiency is book-, league-, market-, and even time-of-day-dependent. That is exactly why your platform's trusted-CLV subset and the BSP-vs-true-sharp caveat matter: even Betfair BSP vs a true sharp close showed only marginal, CI-straddling-zero differences on 123k markets.

## 2. The devig method landscape

All methods map booked probabilities π_i (Σπ_i = 1 + margin) to fair p_i (Σp_i = 1). They differ in *where they put the margin*, which is really a model of the favourite-longshot bias (FLB) — bettors systematically overvalue longshots, so books load proportionally more margin onto them (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favourite-longshot_bias).

- **Multiplicative (basic normalization):** p_i = π_i/Σπ_j. Removes margin equally in *proportional* terms → assumes zero FLB. Fast, wrong in the tails: it leaves longshots overpriced (fair prob too high) and shaves favourites too much. Clarke et al.: "the multiplicative or normalization method performed badly on all predictive performance measures" (https://outlier.bet/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2017-clarke-adjusting_bookmakers_odds.pdf).
- **Additive:** p_i = π_i − margin/n. Removes equal *absolute* probability per outcome → over-corrects longshots to the point of producing **negative probabilities** (the `implied` vignette demonstrates this on a 6-runner field: two runners go negative; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html). Surprisingly competitive on RMSE in Clarke et al.'s tests, but unusable as a general tool because of the range violation.
- **Power:** solve k such that Σπ_i^k = 1 (equivalently p_i = π_i^(1/λ)). Concave transform takes proportionally more probability off longshots → encodes FLB; never leaves [0,1]. **Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram (2017)**, on three large datasets (incl. ATP tennis from actual bookmaker odds), found power "universally outperforms the multiplicative method and outperforms or is comparable to the Shin method," and beat everything on the ATP set (URL above). This is why power is your platform's correct default.
- **Shin (1992, *Economic Journal* 102:426–435, jstor.org/stable/2234526):** structural insider-trading model — a fraction z of money is from bettors who *know* the outcome; the bookmaker prices to protect against them, which endogenously generates FLB. Solve for z from the overround, invert for p_i. Štrumbelj (2014) showed Shin-devigged probabilities beat basic normalization and regression-based mappings on large football/basketball samples (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207014000533). Originally numerical; Kizildemir, Akin & Alkan (2025, *JQAS* 21(2):153–158) reduced it to an analytical solution, implemented as `efficient_shin_conversion` in the goto_conversion package (https://github.com/gotoConversion/goto_conversion). Best suited to multi-runner/insider-prone markets (racing, tennis outrights).
- **'bb' balanced books:** same insider-fraction Z setup as Shin but different bookmaker objective — Shin's book *maximizes profit* facing insiders; balanced-book *minimizes worst-case loss* if the least likely outcome lands (`implied` vignette, URL above). Rarely the winner in comparisons; useful as a robustness check.
- **Odds-ratio (Cheung):** posits OR(π_i, p_i) = c, i.e., π_i(1−p_i)/(p_i(1−π_i)) constant across outcomes — margin applied on the odds-ratio scale, another FLB-respecting concave map (`implied` vignette; Buchdahl implements it as one of his three "disproportionate" methods). In Buchdahl's *Wisdom of the Crowd* study, of the candidate models tested against actual returns at Pinnacle, "the 'odds ratio' model appears to fit expectation best of all, although it's impossible to determine if that is simply the result of chance," while the **equal-margin (multiplicative) model was affirmatively rejected** — his Sampdoria example shows equal-margin implying "true" odds of 12.8 (value at 13) where margin-proportional/odds-ratio/logarithmic imply 13.9/13.3/13.8 (no value) (https://www.football-data.co.uk/The_Wisdom_of_the_Crowd_updated.pdf). His "margin proportional to odds" (m_i scaled by n·margin·(1/odds_i)... his MPTO formula) and "logarithmic function" (p_i = π_i^(1/n) — i.e., the power method under another name) round out the family; he notes the logarithmic/power form "accords with much standard economic theory of risk management."
- **goto_conversion (implied-SE shrink):** reduces every inverse-odds by the *same number of standard-error units*, where SE_i = √(π_i(1−π_i)) is proportionally wider for longshots — so equal-SE shrinkage removes proportionally more from longshots, handling FLB without iteration. Empirical support is Kaggle-competitive rather than peer-reviewed ($47k prize money, 10+ golds; used in a March Madness winning solution), claiming better accuracy than Shin and power on those tasks (https://github.com/gotoConversion/goto_conversion; https://pypi.org/project/goto-conversion/0.1.0). Your own walk-forward validation (single-shot on fresh 2026 BSP data, ADR-0021) is the right epistemic standard here — treat the Kaggle results as promising, not established.

**FLB handling scorecard (established):** multiplicative = none; additive = overshoots; power/logarithmic, odds-ratio, Shin, goto_conversion, MPTO = all encode it, with power ≥ Shin ≥ multiplicative the most replicated ordering (Clarke et al.), Shin > basic on forecast accuracy (Štrumbelj), and odds-ratio best-fitting Buchdahl's Pinnacle returns data.

## 3. Log-ratio vs percent CLV, and why you devig the close

Percent CLV (Unabated's house method) is (p_close − p_bet)/p_bet on implied probabilities; their worked example: bet −195 (66.1%), close −220 (68.8%) → 2.7pt/66.1% ≈ 4% — but after devigging the close (−220 → fair −199.4) the honest CLV is **under 1%**, "which also represents our expected value on the play" (https://unabated.com/articles/getting-precise-about-closing-line-value). Two lessons they make explicit: (a) raw-vs-raw CLV overstates by roughly the vig (~4.5% typical, market-dependent), and if you don't devig you must subtract it; (b) devigged CLV *is* your EV estimate, which is the whole point.

The log formulation, CLV_log = ln(p_close_novig / p_bet_novig), is preferred for aggregation: it is symmetric (a +10% and −10% move cancel), additive across picks, stake-weightable, and doesn't understate value at long prices the way raw decimal-odds differences do. Percent CLV compresses at the tails; log CLV is the same object as the log-score improvement the close achieves over your price, which is why it correlates with long-run ROI. (Formulation per the Unabated method refined to logs; this is also the internal standard in your clv-evaluation skill and Buchdahl's expected-profit-from-close ratio is its exponential: taken_odds/fair_close_odds − 1 ≈ CLV_log for small values — https://www.football-data.co.uk/blog/wisdom_of_crowd_betting_system_closing_odds.php.)

**Critical mechanical rule:** devig the *full market structure* on both sides with the *same method*. Mixing methods (power at bet time, multiplicative at close) injects a systematic bias of the same order as the edges you're hunting, and the direction of the bias flips between favourites and dogs.

## 4. Pitfalls

- **Consensus-as-close.** A consensus of soft books is not a sharp close: Kaunitz et al. showed the consensus close itself defines exploitable outliers — meaning consensus contains the mispricings you're supposed to measure against (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02824). Angelini & De Angelis show book-level closes differ systematically in efficiency (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p712-721.html). Benchmark against Pinnacle/exchange or don't call it CLV. (Your clv-evidence-reviewer trusted-subset discipline is exactly this.)
- **Vig asymmetry across markets.** Margin percentage and its *tilt* differ by market (ML vs totals vs props) and by three-way vs two-way structure — Unabated flags ~4.5% "but that can vary based on the type of bet." Comparing raw CLV across markets confounds vig structure with skill; devig per-market, aggregate per (league, market, book).
- **2-way vs 3-way.** In a 3-way (1X2) market the margin is spread over three outcomes and FLB tilt matters more (the draw and dog carry disproportionate margin); devigging a 1X2 close with multiplicative overstates fair longshot probability and fabricates positive CLV on dogs. Also never devig a 2-way derived market (e.g., DNB) with the 3-way structure or vice versa — the margin allocation differs. Buchdahl's Sampdoria example is the canonical demonstration that method choice alone flips a bet from "+1.6% value" to negative (https://www.football-data.co.uk/The_Wisdom_of_the_Crowd_updated.pdf).
- **Method range violations.** Additive can output negative probabilities on longshot-heavy fields; Clarke et al. also show >1 pathologies for multiplicative/Shin on extreme short favourites (https://outlier.bet/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2017-clarke-adjusting_bookmakers_odds.pdf; https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html). Guard rails belong in the pure-math layer.
- **Timestamp integrity.** Simon (2024) shows the last pre-game price can be *worse* than the price 90 minutes prior at some books/slots — "the close" you record must be provenance-stamped (source, time, market state), or your benchmark quietly degrades (figshare link above).

## (a) Five sharpest takeaways

1. **The sharp close is the best cheap benchmark, not an oracle.** Simon 2024 (Mgmt Sci) shows non-monotone forecast improvement and exploitable pre-close windows even at real sportsbooks; benchmark against a provenance-stamped sharp close, never a soft/consensus close.
2. **Power devig is the defensible default; Shin for multi-runner/insider-heavy markets.** Clarke et al. 2017: power ≥ Shin ≫ multiplicative across three sports; Štrumbelj 2014: Shin > normalization for forecast accuracy.
3. **Never use multiplicative for value detection.** Buchdahl showed equal-margin devig manufactures phantom value on longshots (Sampdoria: +1.6% "value" flips negative under any FLB-aware method).
4. **Devigged log-CLV = your EV estimate.** Raw-line CLV overstates by ~the vig; devig both sides with the same method, compute ln(p_close/p_bet), aggregate stake-weighted by (league, market, book).
5. **goto_conversion's equal-SE-shrink is an elegant non-iterative FLB correction with strong competitive (Kaggle) but thin peer-reviewed support** — exactly the class of method to adopt only behind pre-registered single-shot validation, as you did.

## (b) Unknown / contested

- **How much closing-line inefficiency survives out-of-sample and post-limiting.** Simon's MLB profits and Angelini & De Angelis's mispriced bookmakers are historical/in-sample; Kaunitz et al.'s real-money run ended by limiting. No 2023–2026 study demonstrates *persistent, currently-executable* profit against a Pinnacle-class close.
- **Which FLB-aware devig is "true."** Power, odds-ratio, Shin, goto_conversion are near-indistinguishable on typical 2/3-way markets; Buchdahl explicitly couldn't rule out chance in odds-ratio's win, and rankings flip by sport/dataset (Shin edged power on greyhound RMSE). The margin-allocation function books actually use is unobservable and likely book- and market-specific.
- **Exchange (BSP) vs sharp-book close as the better truth proxy.** Štrumbelj found exchanges not always best in small markets; your own 123k-market BSP study left the sharp-vs-BSP gap CI-straddling zero.
- **Whether CLV thresholds transfer across market types** (props/derivatives vs main lines) — vig tilt and liquidity differ enough that a universal "beat the no-vig close X% of the time" bar is folklore, not established.
- **goto_conversion's superiority claim** over Shin/power awaits peer-reviewed, walk-forward confirmation outside Kaggle metrics.

# Kelly Criterion in Practice — Deep Briefing

## 1. The log-growth derivation and why full Kelly is asymptotically optimal

Kelly (1956) showed that a gambler with repeated favorable opportunities should choose stakes to maximize `E[log(wealth)]`, because with multiplicative compounding the exponential growth rate of capital `G = lim (1/N) log(V_N/V_0)` is what the strong law of large numbers actually controls; maximizing `E[log]` per bet maximizes this a.s. growth rate, and in his channel formulation the maximum growth rate equals the information transmission rate (https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/kelly_56.pdf). For a binary bet at net odds `b` (decimal odds `d`, `b = d−1`) with win probability `p`, the maximizer of `p·log(1+fb) + (1−p)·log(1−f)` is the familiar `f* = (bp − q)/b = (dp − 1)/(d − 1)`.

The asymptotic optimality claims are precise, not folklore. MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba's "Good and Bad Properties of the Kelly Criterion" catalogs the Breiman (1960/61) results: the E-log bettor (i) has a.s. higher long-run growth than any essentially different strategy, (ii) asymptotically dominates in wealth ratio, and (iii) minimizes expected time to reach large wealth targets (https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf). The same source states the key overbetting fact: in the continuous (lognormal) approximation, growth is `g(c) = g*·c(2−c)` when you bet `c` times Kelly, so growth hits **zero at exactly 2× Kelly** — betting more than Kelly strictly increases risk while strictly decreasing growth, i.e., everything above `c=1` is dominated. The "bad properties" side is equally established: Kelly stakes are enormous when edges are large ("essentially different from expected-utility optimization for finite horizons"), wealth paths are violently volatile, and asymptotic dominance says nothing about your next 500 bets.

## 2. Drawdown mathematics: full vs fractional

Thorp's handbook chapter "The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting, and the Stock Market" gives the workhorse formulas (https://knowen-production.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/attachment/file/548/www.edwardothorp.com_sitebuildercontent_sitebuilderfiles_KellyCriterion2007.pdf). In the continuous approximation with drift `m` and variance `s²` per unit time, the probability of **ever** being reduced to a fraction `x` of the initial bankroll is

`P(min V ≤ x·V₀) = x^(2m/s² − 1)` → and at the full-Kelly fraction this exponent is 1, so **P = x exactly**: a full-Kelly bettor halves the bankroll at some point with probability 1/2, loses 90% with probability 0.1 (Thorp, §3.2 and §7, same URL). Betting a fraction `c` of Kelly, the exponent becomes `2/c − 1`, giving the practitioner's table:

| c (× Kelly) | P(ever halve) | Relative growth `c(2−c)` |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1/2 | 1.00 |
| 0.5 | (1/2)³ = 12.5% | 0.75 |
| 0.25 | (1/2)⁷ ≈ 0.8% | 0.4375 |

MacLean & Ziemba's discrete blackjack computation matches: P(doubling before halving) is 0.67 at full Kelly, 0.89 at half, 0.98 at 0.3 Kelly, and drops to 0.50 with zero growth at 2× Kelly (table reproduced in https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf).

**Busseti, Ryu & Boyd (2016)** turned this from formula into constraint (https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06183; full PDF https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf). Their Risk-Constrained Kelly (RCK) problem maximizes `E log(rᵀb)` subject to `Prob(W_min < α) ≤ β`. The exact constraint is intractable, but the bound `E[(rᵀb)^(−λ)] ≤ 1` with `λ = log β / log α` is convex and **guarantees** the drawdown constraint holds; the whole thing solves as a small convex program and admits arbitrary convex constraints on `b` (limits, caps). Two practical findings from their experiments: RCK dominates its quadratic (Markowitz-like) approximation QRCK, and in their return-distribution example **fractional Kelly traced essentially the same risk–growth frontier as RCK** — quantitative support for the practitioner shortcut, with RCK being the principled tool when you need a certified `(α, β)` (e.g., "≤5% chance of ever losing 30%").

## 3. Fractional Kelly as shrinkage under parameter uncertainty

The deepest justification for fractional Kelly is not risk appetite but **estimation error**. Baker & McHale (2013), "Optimal Betting Under Parameter Uncertainty: Improving the Kelly Criterion" (Decision Analysis 10(3), https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/deca.2013.0271), model the win probability as estimated with standard deviation σ and show that for log utility **the optimal correction is always shrinkage** of the stake; naive plug-in Kelly systematically overstates achievable expected utility because you bet biggest exactly where the estimate is most favorable (a winner's-curse effect). They derive/compare several shrinkage-factor estimators — one is an explicit "back-of-envelope" multiplier a bettor can apply by hand, a decreasing function of σ relative to the edge — and validate on simulations and tennis data that shrunken Kelly beats raw Kelly out-of-sample (abstract and takeaways: https://www.academia.edu/20341527/Optimal_Betting_Under_Parameter_Uncertainty_Improving_the_Kelly_Criterion). A caveat they flag: for non-log (less risk-averse) utilities and very favorable odds, the correction can occasionally be a *swelling*, but shrinkage is the generic case.

Three complements. (a) In continuous time, fractional Kelly `c` is *exactly* optimal for a negative-power utility `w^δ/δ` with `c = 1/(1−δ)` — so half-Kelly = crra 2 — but this equivalence is only approximate in discrete settings (MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba, Berkeley PDF above; Kadane 2011 "Partial-Kelly Strategies and Expected Utility: Small-Edge Asymptotics" formalizes the small-edge limit, cited at https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ordeca/v5y2008i1p10-18.html). (b) **Distributionally robust Kelly** (Sun & Boyd 2018) maximizes worst-case log-growth over an ambiguity set of outcome distributions — a principled multiplicative-uncertainty shrinkage that stays convex (https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/robust_kelly.pdf). (c) Uhrín, Šourek, Hubáček & Železný's experimental review implemented exactly this as "KellyRobust" and found it the most conservative-but-never-ruined strategy on real horse/basketball/football data (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827).

## 4. Simultaneous / portfolio Kelly

Concurrent bets break single-bet Kelly because stakes compete for the same bankroll and outcomes may correlate. **Whitrow (2007)**, "Algorithms for optimal allocation of bets on many simultaneous events" (JRSS-C 56(5), https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9876.2007.00594.x), generalizes Kelly staking to many simultaneous bets under log-utility; exact solution requires enumerating the joint outcome space (2ⁿ for n independent binaries), so he develops numerical/approximate algorithms. **Grant, Johnstone & Kwon (2008)**, "Optimal Betting Strategies for Simultaneous Games" (Decision Analysis 5(1), https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ordeca/v5y2008i1p10-18.html), solve the case where the bookmaker also accepts bets on joint outcomes (parlays) of subsets of games — with a full menu of exotic bets the problem decomposes cleanly, and parlay availability changes optimal single-game stakes. Thorp's chapter adds two field notes: correlation matters enormously (his Table 5 shows `f*` *increasing* as correlation decreases), for exact solutions "covariance information is not enough" — you need the joint distribution — and in his own sports operation, bets on different games were near-independent and few enough that per-bet fractions were "only moderately less" than single-bet Kelly (Thorp PDF above).

For scale, the standard engineering move is the **quadratic approximation** `E log(1+R) ≈ E[R] − ½E[R²]`, turning simultaneous Kelly into a Markowitz-style QP — this is precisely Busseti et al.'s QRCK, and their result that RCK (exact convex program) beats QRCK is a warning that the QP shortcut costs real growth when payoffs are skewed, as longshot bets are (https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf). Comparative evidence across exact simultaneous Kelly and its approximations is collected in "A Comparison of Simultaneous Kelly Betting Strategies" (Journal of Gambling Business and Economics, https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jgbe/article/view/579/620).

## 5. Commission and liquidity limits

Exchange commission is handled by repricing, not by hacking the formula: effective decimal odds = `1 + (1 − c)·(odds − 1)` (Smarkets help, https://help.smarkets.com/hc/en-gb/articles/115003543125-How-to-calculate-betting-exchange-commission-into-odds). Compute edge and Kelly on the **net** `b_eff = (1−c)(d−1)`; a bet that is +EV gross can be −EV net, so the commission adjustment belongs *before* the edge gate, not just in staking. Liquidity caps (limited depth at the top of the book) are convex constraints, so they slot directly into the Busseti-style convex program; because `E log` is concave in the stake, a binding cap simply truncates the stake at the cap for a single bet — but with multiple simultaneous bets a cap on one leg re-inflates optimal stakes elsewhere, so re-solve jointly rather than capping post hoc (constraint framework in https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/kelly.pdf). Walking the book (worse average price as you consume depth) makes realized `b` a decreasing function of stake — a further concave penalty that shrinks optimal size below the naive cap; this last point is standard reasoning rather than a published theorem.

## 6. Practitioner ladders and when flat staking wins

Half- and quarter-Kelly are folklore defaults, but the numbers behind them are established: half-Kelly keeps 75% of growth while cutting P(ever halving) from 50% to 12.5%; quarter keeps ~44% with sub-1% halving risk (Thorp formulas above; ladder discussion in MacLean/Ziemba/Blazenko lineage cited by https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827). The strongest *empirical* evidence for aggressive fractioning under real model error is Uhrín et al.'s review: on real football data with a genuine +EV model, **raw plug-in Kelly and raw max-Sharpe went to ruin in 100% of simulation runs** (median terminal wealth ~10⁻⁹), while fractional Kelly (ω tuned ~0.1–0.3), Kelly-with-drawdown-constraint, and robust Kelly all avoided ruin and multiplied bankroll ~10× median (Table 7, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827). That is the estimation-error regime in one table: the "optimal" strategy is the worst strategy the moment probabilities are estimates.

Flat staking is the `σ → large` limit of this logic. Baker–McHale shrinkage goes to ~0 as probability-estimate variance swamps the edge; when your per-pick edge estimates are noisy enough that their *ranking* is untrustworthy, proportional staking concentrates bankroll on the most-overestimated edges (the same winner's-curse that shows up as negative CLV in high-apparent-edge/high-odds tails), while flat stakes are immune to that selection effect. Established: shrinkage always helps under uncertainty (Baker–McHale); raw Kelly is catastrophic with estimated inputs (Uhrín et al.). Folklore: any specific crossover point ("flat beats Kelly when edge-noise > X") — no clean published threshold exists; it depends on the joint distribution of true vs estimated edges, which is exactly what CLV tracking estimates.

## (a) Five sharpest takeaways

1. **Full Kelly's drawdown law is brutal and exact**: P(ever reaching fraction x of bankroll) = x at full Kelly, x^(2/c−1) at fraction c — half-Kelly buys a 4× reduction in halving risk for 25% of growth (Thorp, https://knowen-production.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/attachment/file/548/www.edwardothorp.com_sitebuildercontent_sitebuilderfiles_KellyCriterion2007.pdf).
2. **Fractional Kelly is shrinkage, not cowardice**: under parameter uncertainty the log-optimal act is to shrink the plug-in stake, with the factor driven by estimate variance vs edge (Baker & McHale 2013, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/deca.2013.0271).
3. **Never exceed Kelly, ever**: growth is `c(2−c)` — zero at 2×, and all `c > 1` is dominated in both growth and risk (https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf).
4. **If you want a certified drawdown guarantee, use RCK**: `E[(rᵀb)^(−λ)] ≤ 1`, λ = log β/log α, is a convex constraint that provably bounds P(drawdown), accommodates liquidity caps, and empirically fractional Kelly hugs its frontier (https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06183).
5. **With real estimated probabilities, raw Kelly ruins you**: 100% ruin rate on real football data vs ~10× median growth for fractional/robust variants — size the fraction to your measured estimation error (CLV residuals), not to a ladder convention (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08827).

## (b) Unknown / contested

- **The right fraction**: no theory pins c for a given estimation-error profile outside stylized models; Baker–McHale's factor needs σ(p̂), which most operators can only proxy (e.g., from CLV dispersion). The quarter/half ladder is convention, not derivation.
- **Flat-vs-Kelly crossover**: no published clean threshold for when edge-estimate noise makes flat staking dominate fractional Kelly; evidence is simulation-specific.
- **Simultaneous Kelly at scale with correlation**: exact solutions need the joint outcome distribution ("covariance is not enough" — Thorp); practical correlation modeling for same-league/same-day bets remains ad hoc, and QP approximations demonstrably lose growth on skewed payoffs (Busseti et al.).
- **Bayesian vs multiplicative shrinkage**: whether full posterior-averaged Kelly (Browne–Whitt lineage), Baker–McHale scalar shrinkage, or distributionally-robust Kelly (https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/pdf/robust_kelly.pdf) wins out-of-sample in sports markets is unresolved; Uhrín et al. found robust Kelly safest but slowest-growing.
- **Non-log terminal preferences**: for finite horizons Kelly is not expected-utility optimal for anyone whose utility isn't log; how much that matters over a betting career of ~10³–10⁴ bets (where asymptotics haven't kicked in) is quantitatively open (MacLean/Thorp/Ziemba "bad properties").

# Backtest Validity for Betting Systems — Deep Briefing

Prepared 2026-07-10 for the operator of a sharp-anchored, CLV-tracked picks platform. Sources read in full or substantial part: Bailey/Borwein/López de Prado/Zhu (PBO), Bailey/López de Prado (DSR), Kaunitz et al., Zimmermann (IDA 2024), Wunderlich dissertation (containing Wunderlich & Memmert 2020), Harvey & Liu; plus verified abstracts for Walsh & Joshi and Kapoor & Narayanan.

## 1. Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) and CSCV mechanics

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu formalize the core problem: when N strategy variants are tried on the same history, the best in-sample (IS) performer is selected partly for fitting noise, and "standard statistical techniques designed to prevent regression overfitting, such as hold-out, tend to be unreliable and inaccurate in the context of investment backtests" (paper: https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf; published J. Computational Finance 2017, record: https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/42).

CSCV mechanics, concretely: build the T×N matrix of per-period returns for all N trials (for you: per-bet or per-day P&L for every strategy/gate/market variant scanned). Partition rows into S equal blocks. Form all C(S, S/2) ways to pick half the blocks as "IS" and half as "OOS" — the combinatorial symmetry means every observation appears equally often on both sides, unlike a single hold-out. In each split: rank trials by IS Sharpe (or ROI), take the IS winner, find its **relative rank ω̄ among the N trials OOS**, and compute the logit λ = ln(ω̄/(1−ω̄)). **PBO = the fraction of splits where λ < 0**, i.e., the rate at which the IS-optimal strategy underperforms the *median* of trials OOS — the paper's explicit analogy is the placebo arm in a clinical trial. The framework also yields performance-degradation regression (IS vs OOS SR), probability of OOS loss, and a stochastic-dominance check of whether your selection procedure beats picking a config at random. In their random-walk seasonal-strategy example, all IS Sharpes were 1–2.2 yet ~53% of OOS Sharpes were negative and PBO ≈ 55% — the signature of pure selection bias. Key property for your pipeline: CSCV is model-free, nonparametric, and needs only the trial-returns matrix — you can bolt it onto the existing walk-forward harness as a second gate whenever a "best variant" is chosen from a scan.

Caveat (from the same paper): PBO measures *selection-induced* inflation, not whether the strategy is good — φ ≈ 0 can coexist with poor absolute OOS performance.

## 2. Deflated Sharpe Ratio and its betting analogue

Bailey & López de Prado's DSR (https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf; JPM 40(5):94, https://www.pm-research.com/content/iijpormgmt/40/5/94) is a Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio whose rejection threshold is raised from 0 to the **expected maximum SR under the null of zero skill across N trials**, computed via the Euler–Mascheroni approximation: SR₀ ≈ √V[{SR̂ₙ}]·[(1−γ)·Z⁻¹(1−1/N) + γ·Z⁻¹(1−1/(Ne))]. The PSR itself corrects for track length T, skewness γ₃ and kurtosis γ₄ of returns: DSR = Z[(SR̂ − SR₀)√(T−1) / √(1 − γ₃SR̂ + ((γ₄−1)/4)SR̂²)]. Inputs you must therefore log *by construction*: number of (quasi-)independent trials, variance of performance across trials, T, and return moments.

**Betting analogue** (synthesis, not in the paper): apply DSR to the per-bet flat-stake return series. Two adaptations matter. (a) Non-normality is far worse than in daily equity returns — a bet at decimal odds d has a two-point payout distribution; at d = 4.0 skewness is large and the (γ₃, γ₄) correction bites hard, so a raw t-stat on ROI overstates significance for longshot-tilted books. (b) N is your *scan width*: every league × market × gate-threshold combination examined counts as a trial (correlated trials shrink effective N — DSR handles this crudely through V[{SR̂ₙ}]; Harvey & Liu handle it explicitly, §7). Related and directly usable: the same group's Minimum Backtest Length in "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism" (Notices of the AMS, 2014, https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf): since E[max SR] under the null grows like √(2 ln N), required history grows with ln N — hold-out length must scale with how much you searched.

## 3. Returns vs accuracy vs calibration as selection criteria

**Wunderlich & Memmert 2020** (Int. J. Forecasting 36(2):713–722, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016920701930233X; full text inside the Wunderlich dissertation, https://fis-db.dshs-koeln.de/ws/portalfiles/portal/6465170/Dissertation_Wunderlich.pdf): via simulation controlling bookmaker error, bettor error, and the **correlation between the two error processes**, they show betting returns are "an adequate measure of profitability" but **not a valid measure of model accuracy**. Central results: returns *fall* as bettor–bookmaker error correlation rises (mispricings are only findable when errors point in opposite directions); a model can be profitable while being *less* accurate than the bookmaker; larger bookmaker errors raise returns even when the bettor's errors grow equally; and their "profitability paradox" — two actors' models can each profitably exploit the other. Operational reading for you: a positive-ROI backtest is evidence about *decorrelation from the market you priced against*, not about probability quality — which is why ROI against soft books and calibration/CLV against a sharp anchor are separate axes and must both be tracked.

**Walsh & Joshi 2024** (Machine Learning with Applications 16:100539, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S266682702400015X; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021) complete the triangle: selecting NBA models on **calibration** rather than accuracy produced profitable betting while the accuracy-selected model lost money — "accuracy alone is not a sufficient model evaluation metric for the sports betting problem."

**Zimmermann 2024** (IDA 2024, "Learning predictive models for match outcomes in US sports, and using them to bet," https://zimmermanna.users.greyc.fr/papers/conferences/main-ida24.pdf) supplies the empirical pitfall catalogue from actually betting NCAAB/NBA/NFL models: high accuracy ≠ high payout because *which* matchups you get right dominates (always-picking-favorites hit ~74% in the NCAAB post-season yet paid ~$30; five coin-flip "Pick 'ems" alone could swing the book ±$470); following the money line over a full NBA season yields "very respectable accuracy but also significant monetary loss"; and some pitfalls (odds availability, small high-variance subsets) "are harder to avoid than others" in live deployment. Note: the arXiv 2410.21484 "Systematic Review of ML in Sports Betting" (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21484v1) is a different, shallower survey — the Zimmermann IDA paper is the one with the evaluation-pitfall content.

## 4. Walk-forward vs k-fold for temporally ordered odds data

Established: naive k-fold on temporally ordered betting data leaks — future matches sit in training folds while past matches are tested, and betting features (rolling team form, Elo, league tables, market-drift stats) are cumulative functions of history, so shuffling destroys the information-arrival structure. Kapoor & Narayanan's leakage taxonomy ("Leakage and the Reproducibility Crisis in ML-based Science," https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048, published in Patterns 2023) names this class **temporal leakage / no clean train-test separation** and documents it across 17 fields (294 papers affected). López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (Wiley 2018) contributes the standard middle path: **purged k-fold with embargo** (drop training observations whose information window overlaps the test window, plus an embargo buffer) and **combinatorial purged CV (CPCV)**, which generates many OOS paths rather than walk-forward's single path. Practical hierarchy for your data: walk-forward is the honest default (it reproduces deployment), but it is one path and its verdict depends on regime ordering; CSCV/CPCV on top of walk-forward variant scans gives you the selection-bias correction walk-forward alone cannot. Bergmeir & Benítez (Information Sciences 2012, "On the use of cross-validation for time series predictor evaluation") show blocked CV can be valid for *purely autoregressive, stationary* setups — a condition betting markets fail (market sharpening, rule changes, vig regimes), so treat that result as inapplicable folklore when quoted at you.

## 5. Betting-specific leakage taxonomy

Partly literature, partly hard-won practitioner knowledge — flagged accordingly:

- **Closing odds in features or as executable prices** (documented): Kaunitz et al. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02824) show the gap concretely — the same strategy returned 3.5% on *closing* odds (56,435 bets, 2005–2015) but 9.9% on *continuous* pre-close odds (6,994 bets): the price you model and the price you can hit are different objects, in either direction. Any feature computed at or after close (closing consensus, final line move) is leakage for a bet placed earlier.
- **Stale/phantom max odds** (folklore, universally accepted among practitioners): backtesting at the archive's "best available odds" assumes you could always hit the top-of-market quote; those quotes are often stale, size-limited, or from limit-happy books. Kaunitz et al. quantified an adjacent effect — randomly discarding 30% of games (odds gone by execution time) still left profit, but they had to test it because the naive backtest ignores it. Their real-money coda is the sharpest survivorship lesson in the literature: 8.5% ROI over 265 bets and then **accounts limited within months** — the market's response function is absent from every backtest.
- **Settlement leakage** (practitioner): grading rules (voids, pushes, OT rules, retirement rules, dead-heat) applied with hindsight or with the wrong book's rules inflate ROI; result feeds keyed on post-hoc corrected scores can settle bets that would have graded differently in real time. (Your own tennis-retirement and residual-grade fixes are instances.)
- **Survivorship in odds archives** (practitioner): archives disproportionately retain matched/settled, liquid, big-league events; scrape gaps are non-random (blackouts correlate with volatile periods). A model trained/evaluated only on surviving rows sees a cleaner market than the one you bet into.
- **Label/feature recomputation leakage**: team-strength tables rebuilt from full-season data, player ratings back-filled after transfers — Kapoor & Narayanan's "illegitimate features" class.

## 6. Pre-registration and single-shot protocols

The PBO paper's demonstration that hold-out is "unreliable" *once iterated against* has a precise information-theoretic backing: Dwork et al., "The reusable holdout: preserving validity in adaptive data analysis" (Science 349:636–638, 2015, https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaa9375) — each adaptive query against a holdout spends validity, and only noise-adding mechanisms preserve it. The clean practical protocol is pre-registration: fix hypothesis, parameters, sample, and decision rule *before* the evaluation data exists, then evaluate once (Nosek et al., "The preregistration revolution," PNAS 115:2600–2606, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1708274114). Your existing regime — parameters frozen in a signed ADR, single-shot evaluation on a fresh BSP tar, refusal to re-tune on a spent holdout — is exactly the transfer of this into betting; the literature's addition is that a *partially* spent holdout can be salvaged only with thresholded/noisy readouts, never with full metric disclosure.

## 7. Sample-size math for a 2–4% edge (derivation, arithmetic not citation)

Flat 1-unit stakes at decimal odds d, win prob p, per-bet return X ∈ {d−1, −1}: Var(X) = p(1−p)d². At d = 2.0 (p≈0.5), σ ≈ 1.0 units; σ grows roughly like √(d−1) — at d = 4.0, σ ≈ 1.7. Two-sided α = 0.05, power 80%: n = ((z₀.₉₇₅+z₀.₈)·σ/μ)² = (2.80·σ/μ)². Thus at even odds: **2% edge → ~19,600 bets; 3% → ~8,700; 4% → ~4,900**. At d = 4.0 multiply by ~3 (≈57k bets for 2%). Equivalent bootstrap view: the 95% CI half-width on ROI is ≈1.96σ/√n — ±1% at even odds needs n ≈ 38,000; ±2% needs ~9,600. This is why season-scale ROI (hundreds to low-thousands of bets) is statistically vacuous for 2–4% edges — consistent with Pinnacle's practitioner treatment (https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/educational/sample-size-needed-to-evaluate-betting-trends) and with sportsbook-monitor rules of thumb that ~200-bet records need >60% win rates to mean anything (https://www.sportsinsights.com/sports-investing-statistical-significance). The escape hatch your platform already uses: **CLV as a low-variance surrogate** — per-bet log-CLV has σ of a few percent rather than ~100%, cutting required n by 2–3 orders of magnitude; its validity rests on the closing-odds-efficiency literature (Kaunitz et al.'s consensus-close assumption) and is *established as an indicator, not a theorem*.

## 8. Multiple comparisons across leagues/markets

Scanning L leagues × M markets × K thresholds is a multiple-testing family. Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting" (J. Portfolio Management 2015; SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2345489; PDF https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P120_Backtesting.PDF) replace the folklore "halve the Sharpe" with a principled **haircut**: compute a multiple-testing-adjusted p-value (Bonferroni/Holm for FWER, or Benjamini–Hochberg–Yekutieli for FDR — BHY preferred under correlated tests), then back out the equivalent single-test Sharpe; haircuts are nonlinear — marginal strategies get destroyed, strong ones survive. Their companion cross-section work argues new discoveries should clear t ≈ 3.0, not 2.0. For a league/market scan the FDR framing (Benjamini & Hochberg 1995, JRSS-B 57:289–300) is the right default: you don't need zero false positives, you need the *fraction* of promoted strategies that are noise controlled at, say, 10% — and effective test count must be shrunk for correlation between overlapping markets (h2h vs AH vs totals on the same fixtures are far from independent tests).

## (a) Five sharpest takeaways

1. **Log every trial or your statistics are fiction.** PBO, DSR, and Harvey–Liu haircuts all require N — the number of variants examined, including abandoned ones. A backtest harness that doesn't persist the full trial matrix cannot, even in principle, tell you whether its winner is real. E[max SR|null] grows ~√(2 ln N), so search width silently manufactures edges.
2. **ROI backtests measure decorrelation from a specific counterparty, not model quality.** (Wunderlich & Memmert). Select models on calibration/CLV against a sharp anchor; use ROI only as the profitability readout against the soft side — and expect the "edge" to be a function of *which* book's errors you're anti-correlated with.
3. **A 2–4% edge is invisible in ROI at realistic sample sizes** (~5k–60k bets for significance depending on edge and odds). CLV-based inference is the only per-quarter-answerable question; settle-based ROI is a multi-year question. Anyone showing you a significant 500-bet ROI is showing you variance or leakage.
4. **Hold-outs are consumables.** Every adaptive look spends validity (Dwork et al.); the only regenerating resources are pre-registered single-shot evaluations on data that didn't exist when the hypothesis was frozen. Walk-forward is necessary but not sufficient — it's one path; layer CSCV/CPCV over variant scans to price the selection step itself.
5. **The deadliest leaks are price-availability leaks, not feature leaks**: closing-vs-executable odds (3.5% vs 9.9% on the *same* strategy in Kaunitz et al.), stale max-odds, and the market's behavioral response (limiting) that no archive records. Backtest at the odds you can actually hit, with an execution-delay/availability haircut, or report both bounds.

## (b) Unknown / contested

- **Effective N under correlation**: no consensus method for counting "independent" trials when scanned strategies overlap 90% (DSR's V[{SR}] proxy vs Harvey–Liu's correlation-adjusted FDR give materially different haircuts); this is the loosest joint in every deflation formula.
- **CLV-as-ground-truth**: closing-line efficiency is well-supported for liquid football/US majors but unproven (and likely weaker) in the obscure-league, low-liquidity segments where soft-book edges concentrate — precisely where you most need the surrogate. How much positive-CLV → positive-ROI transfer degrades with liquidity is not quantified anywhere in the open literature.
- **PBO calibration for betting P&L**: CSCV's block structure assumes exchangeability within blocks; heavy-tailed, heteroskedastic bet returns (mixed odds, mixed stakes) may distort PBO estimates — no published study validates CSCV specifically on sports-betting trial matrices.
- **How much backtest validity survives the market's reaction**: Kaunitz et al.'s limiting experience suggests the binding constraint on soft-book strategies is *access*, not alpha; no framework prices expected account lifetime into strategy evaluation.
- **Walk-forward vs CPCV verdict disagreement rates**: when the two disagree on a betting strategy, which is more predictive of live performance is untested in this domain — the finance evidence (single-path fragility) is suggestive, not dispositive.

# The Economics of Getting Paid: Limiting, Venues, and Where Retail Edges Persist (2024–2026)

## 1. How books detect sharps — what the evidence actually shows

The detection stack is no longer folklore; regulatory proceedings have forced operators to describe it on the record.

**Pattern-of-wagering analytics, not P&L.** At the Massachusetts Gaming Commission's 2024 roundtable, Fanatics' SVP of compliance stated that "nearly half of the small population of limited customers were actually net losing with Fanatics at the time they were limited... we're not looking at the results. We're looking at the way they're wagering" (https://www.espn.com/sports-betting/story/_/id/41231266/espn-sports-betting-news-sportsbooks-defend-practice-limiting-sharp-customers). That is a direct operator admission that limiting keys on *process signals* — CLV-correlated behavior — rather than realized profit. BetMGM told the same hearing it limits ~1% of MA patrons and that this is what "allows us to continue to offer competitive lines... for 99% of non-advantage players."

**Timing and structure.** Since Massachusetts began forcing individualized explanations (June 2026), the notices themselves have become evidence: DraftKings' Massachusetts limit notices reference "timing-based concerns and wagering structure" (https://www.gamingintelligence.com/sectors/betting/231532-massachusetts-first-to-require-sportsbooks-to-justify-account-limits-on-winning-bettors). DraftKings' own FY2023 10-K language frames limiting as protection against "highly sophisticated syndicates and algorithmic bettors, or bettors looking to take advantage of errors and omissions" (quoted in https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/new-york-fair-play-act-sportsbooks-limiting-ban).

**The practitioner-consensus signal list** (consistent across advantage-play guides, e.g. https://betherosports.com/blog/how-to-avoid-sportsbook-limits): (1) systematic positive CLV — universally described as the #1 flag; (2) bet timing — hitting lines immediately on post or just before steam; (3) steam correlation — always being on the same side as sharp moves before they propagate; (4) odd stake sizing (Kelly-looking amounts); (5) concentration in low-limit/derivative markets; (6) immediate withdrawal behavior. Early symptoms of being flagged: acceptance delays, partial fills, shrinking max, promo exclusion. These are practitioner claims rather than audited fact, but they align tightly with the operator statements above — CLV and timing are established; stake-sizing and withdrawal-pattern detection are plausible-but-unverified folklore.

## 2. Measured prevalence and the 2024–2026 regulatory turn

- **WaPo 2022** (Danny Funt, "Sportsbooks say you can win big. Then they try to limit winners.", Nov 17 2022) established the mainstream narrative that major US books systematically cut winners to trivial stakes, with Circa positioned as the exception (https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/2022/11/17/betting-limits-draft-kings-betmgm-caesars-circa).
- **First hard prevalence data:** the MGC study found **0.64% of Massachusetts accounts were limited** (Dec 2024–Sep 2025), and of those, ~58% were restricted to **1–24% of the default bet amount** (https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-bettors-could-soon-have-wagerning-limitations-explained-dec-18-2025). Operator-supplied data confirmed the correlation regulators suspected: "above-average winnings are associated with a higher likelihood of account restrictions" (Gaming Intelligence, above). So: limiting is rare in headcount, near-certain conditional on sustained edge.
- **Massachusetts regulation (effective June 1, 2026)** — the first in the US: operators must notify limited bettors within 48 hours with a *specific, individualized* reason (boilerplate triggers scrutiny), retroactively covering pre-June restrictions (Gaming Intelligence, above). Note what it does **not** do: it mandates transparency, not a limiting ban.
- **New York "Fair Play Act"** (A9125, Assemblymember Alex Bores, Sept 2025): would prohibit limiting/banning customers because they "obtain financial benefit," with RG/integrity carve-outs; sits in the Racing and Wagering Committee (https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/new-york-fair-play-act-sportsbooks-limiting-ban). Unpassed as of mid-2026.
- **Wyoming** investigated and declined to act, concluding limits hit a tiny fraction of accounts, often court-siders/beards (industry discussion: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KPVoIwxDSOw).
- Nothing anywhere in the US/UK/EU as of July 2026 actually *bans* limiting. The realistic 2026–2027 trajectory is Massachusetts-style disclosure spreading, which is operationally useful to you (limit notices = free labels on which of your bet-shapes tripped detection) but does not restore capacity.

## 3. The durable venues

**Exchanges (UK/EU).** Betfair **replaced the 2008-era Premium Charge (up to 60%) with the "Expert Fee" from January 2025**: 0% below £25k gross profit on a rolling 52 weeks, 20% on £25k–£100k, 40% above £100k — on top of standard commission (up to 5%) (https://www.racingpost.com/news/britain/betfair-exchange-to-introduce-new-commission-system-for-2025-as-premium-charge-is-dropped-a7wbg0v4GCAJ; mechanics detail at https://www.sharkbetting.com/guides/betfair-expert-fee). Read this correctly: a winner clearing >£100k/yr on Betfair keeps ~55–60% of marginal gross profit. Smarkets and Matchbook charge flat ~2% with **no** winners' surcharge; Betdaq ~2% (https://punter2pro.com/best-betting-exchange-betfair-comparison). The liquidity reality, long-standing and still true: Betfair dominates; a meaningful share of quotes on smaller exchanges is bots mirroring Betfair with margin added, so "2% commission" venues cannot absorb Betfair-scale size on most markets. Practical consequence: the Expert Fee is effectively the *price of capacity* — you migrate volume to Smarkets/Matchbook until their books thin out, then pay Betfair's tax on the rest.

**Pinnacle-class "winners welcome."** The policy is real and current — Pinnacle publicly maintains it, runs 1.5–3% margins, and posts limits up to ~$100k+ on major markets (https://www.completesports.com/pinnacles-winners-welcome-policy; https://www.sharkbetting.com/blog/sharp-books-explained). But 2025 practitioner reports complicate the marketing: arbusers forum threads describe **customer profiling / stake-factor behavior** — odds moving sharply after small bets from accounts Pinnacle deems sharp, including via brokers (https://arbusers.com/pinnacle-limiting-winners-t10780). Established: Pinnacle won't close or micro-limit your account for winning. Contested: whether your *effective* capacity degrades via aggressive per-account price reaction. Pinnacle is not available in the US.

**Brokers.** BetInAsia (Curacao-licensed) fronts PS3838/Pinnacle, Orbit, Citibet and its BLACK multi-book aggregator; €100 minimum deposit, 3× rollover before fee-free withdrawal, KYC required (https://caanberry.com/betinasia-review; https://blogabet.com/bookmaker/betinasia-review). Sportmarket runs ~€250 minimum with sharp-book aggregation (https://www.betwasp.com/blog/what-is-a-betting-broker-rewiev-popular-brokers); AsianOdds/Asianconnect ~$200. The Asian-book model profits on volume at 1–2% margins, so winners are tolerated structurally. Eligibility caveat: brokers are the legal-gray tier — unavailable/unlawful for US residents and increasingly awkward for UK residents (UKGC-unlicensed operators); counterparty risk sits with the broker, not a regulator.

**US sharp-tolerant venues.** Circa's stated model is *posted uniform limits for everyone*, honored regardless of skill — e.g., NFL sides raised from $20k to $50k on game day (Circa's own account: https://www.circasports.com/blog/art-of-setting-sports-betting-limits). That's genuine but Nevada-anchored, and Nevada is now a rounding error of national handle (~$142.5B legal US handle 2024; Nevada hold leanest at 6.4% — https://utahnewsdispatch.com/2025/02/09/good-sports-how-betting-is-getting-bigger-than-the-game). The p2p exchanges — **Novig** (which also obtained CFTC approval to operate as a prediction-market DCM, https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/167223/novig-ctfc-approval-prediction-market-competition), **ProphetX**, and Sporttrade — market "no limits on winners" explicitly; the honest tradeoff, even per friendly reviews, is thin liquidity outside main markets on featured games (https://xclsvmedia.com/novig-review-2026-peer-to-peer-sportsbook-sharp-bettors). Treat affiliate-site enthusiasm (XCLSV, PropsBot, OddsJam content) as directionally correct on mechanics, unreliable on liquidity depth claims.

**Prediction markets — now the biggest US story.** Per the Congressional Research Service: as of Feb 2026, **~87% of Kalshi's $39.7B trailing-year volume was sports; Polymarket $36.2B with 38% sports** (https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13187). Pew data: combined monthly volume quadrupled from <$5B (Sep 2025) to **$24B (Apr 2026)** (via https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/49019930/cftc-proposes-rules-limiting-prediction-markets-kalshi-sports). Regulatory sequence: CFTC Chair Selig announced rulemaking Jan 2026 → withdrew the 2024 NPRM and Sep 2025 advisory in Feb 2026 → Mar 2026 staff advisory + ANPRM (~3,500 comments) (https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/double-coverage-cftc-exchanges-signal-new-era-of-oversight-for-sports-prediction-markets) → **June 10, 2026: first NPRM**, a 267-page proposal with a three-part public-interest test that would *clear* game-outcome sports contracts while banning injury/officiating-type props; comment window closes ~July 25, 2026 (https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/06/rewriting-the-rulebook-cftc-proposes-rule-changes-for-prediction-market-contracts; ESPN above). Preemption is split: the 3rd Circuit sided with Kalshi against New Jersey (*KalshiEX v. Flaherty*), but a Nevada district court held sports event contracts aren't "swaps" (9th Cir. appeal pending), and a Massachusetts state court enjoined Kalshi's sports contracts in Jan 2026 (Ropes & Gray, above; https://www.betmassachusetts.com). Kalshi/Polymarket do not limit winners — order-book size is the only constraint — which regulators themselves now cite as a driver of sharp migration (https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/massachusetts-wants-good-reason-limiting-235400934.html).

## 4. Realistic capacity ceilings (per venue class, order-of-magnitude)

Sourced anchors: Pinnacle up to ~$250k–$500k majors, $50k–$100k NBA/NFL sides (completesports, above); Circa $50k NFL game-day sides (circasports, above); Betfair main-league match odds routinely absorbs five-to-six figures near the off but taxes >£25k/yr winners 20–40%. Everything else is practitioner estimation: soft-book careers are front-loaded (months, low-six-figures lifetime before micro-limits); Smarkets/Matchbook thousands-per-market outside headline events; brokers/Asian books effectively the highest ceiling for football sides/totals (six figures per match across the network) but with price-impact from Pinnacle-style profiling; US p2p exchanges currently low four figures per market off-peak; Kalshi/Polymarket deep on marquee events (Polymarket World Cup >$2.36B cumulative) and thin on mid-tier markets. Fees, not bans, are the binding constraint at the durable venues.

## 5. Established vs. folklore

**Established:** CLV/timing-based detection (operator statements on record); limiting prevalence ~0.6–1% of accounts but strongly conditional on winning (MGC data); MA disclosure rule live; Betfair Expert Fee terms; broker terms; CRS/Pew prediction-market volumes; June 2026 CFTC NPRM contents. **Folklore/contested:** "Pinnacle never reacts to your account" (contradicted by 2025 practitioner reports); precise soft-book detection features (stake decimals, withdrawal cadence); "no limits" claims on thin p2p books as if liquidity weren't itself a limit; any claim that a US limiting *ban* is imminent.

---

### (a) Five sharpest takeaways
1. **Limiting is triggered by bet-shape, not bankroll damage** — Fanatics: ~half of limited accounts were *net losers* at limiting time; CLV and bet-timing are the operative flags. Any retail-book strategy must budget for detection latency, not detection avoidance.
2. **Massachusetts (June 2026) bought transparency, not capacity** — 48-hour individualized limit notices are now a free diagnostic of which patterns tripped you; NY's Fair Play Act is the only live ban attempt and it's stuck in committee.
3. **The durable venues charge for durability**: Betfair 5% + 20–40% Expert Fee above £25k/£100k rolling profit; Smarkets/Matchbook 2% but Betfair-mirrored thin books; Pinnacle keeps accounts open but 2025 reports suggest per-account price reaction erodes effective capacity.
4. **Prediction markets are now the largest no-limit US venue by an order of magnitude** — ~$35B/yr sports volume on Kalshi alone — and the June 2026 CFTC NPRM would *legitimize* game-outcome contracts (while killing injury/officiating props), with the comment window closing ~July 25, 2026.
5. **Capacity planning beats account preservation**: the realistic career arc is soft-book harvest (short, fee-free) → exchanges/brokers/prediction markets (long, fee-taxed). Model expected fees (2–45% of gross wins depending on venue) into edge thresholds before promotion of any strategy.

### (b) Unknown / contested
- Whether Pinnacle's "winners welcome" survives at scale, or degrades into de-facto stake-factoring for six-figure winners (only forum-grade evidence either way).
- Whether MA-style disclosure spreads to other states and whether it changes limiting *behavior* (MGC itself is waiting on 2026–27 data), and NY Fair Play's fate.
- Final CFTC rule text and the preemption circuit split (3rd Cir. pro-Kalshi vs. Nevada/9th Cir. "not a swap") — a Supreme Court or congressional resolution could re-open or close prediction markets state by state; the Curtis–Schiff bill would ban sports event contracts outright.
- True depth of US p2p exchange liquidity (no audited order-book data; all public claims are affiliate-sourced).
- Whether prediction-market fee structures stay winner-neutral once sharp concentration rises — nothing in the CFTC proposal prohibits a Kalshi-equivalent of the Expert Fee.
